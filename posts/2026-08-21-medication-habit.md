If you've ever gotten to bed and thought, "Wait — did I take my pill this morning?",
you are in very good company. Remembering a daily medication is one of the most common
challenges people face, and it has nothing to do with willpower or memory being bad.
It's just that a new habit hasn't formed yet.

The good news: a few small changes can make taking your heart medication feel as
automatic as brushing your teeth.

## Attach it to something you already do

The easiest habits are the ones that ride along with a habit you already have. This is
sometimes called "anchoring." Instead of trying to remember a new thing out of the
blue, you tie it to something that already happens every day at the same time.

For example:

- Keep your morning medication next to the coffee maker or the kettle.
- Take your evening dose right after you brush your teeth.
- Line it up with feeding a pet or turning on the morning news.

The trick is to pick something you never skip, and let it become the reminder.

## Make it visible

Out of sight really is out of mind. A pill organizer with the days of the week lets you
see at a glance whether today's dose is still sitting there — no guessing, no doubt.
Keeping it somewhere you'll physically see it each day does a lot of the remembering for
you.

> A habit you can see is a habit you're far more likely to keep.

## Be kind to yourself on the misses

Everyone misses a dose sometimes. One missed day is not a failure, and it's not a
reason to give up. What matters is the overall pattern over weeks and months, not any
single day. If you do miss one, don't try to "make up for it" by doubling up — just get
back on track with the next scheduled dose.

## When to check with your care team

A few things are always worth a conversation with your cardiologist or pharmacist:

- If a medication is causing side effects that bother you, don't just stop it — ask
  about it. There are often other options.
- If your schedule is genuinely hard to work around, ask whether a simpler dosing
  routine is possible.
- If you're not sure what a medication is for, ask. Understanding *why* you take
  something makes it much easier to stick with.

Small, steady habits are what protect your heart between visits. You don't have to be
perfect — you just have to keep showing up, one day at a time.
